### 1. The cosmic concept
Galactic filaments and quantum information theory. 
#### Galactic filaments
Galactic filaments being the chains of galaxies that form the web of “bubbles” which the fill the structure of the universe. The filaments of galaxies are particularly graceful, like alien lacework, which is a charmingly dainty metaphor, considering that the filaments are comprised of galaxies containing (by definition) the most violent and energetic phenomenon known to science.
![Portastudio]({{ site.baseurl }}/img/portfolio/galactic filaments-web-scaled.png) Galactic filaments forming a small sliver of the cosmic web. Even though this picture represents an infinitesimal part of the entire cosmos, it is still depicts one of the largest structures of any image you have ever seen.

#### Quantum Information Theory
“Unitarity” is a clumsy word describing the theory that information is never truly lost. It’s a foundational concept also known as the conservation of information.
Quantum information is a more all-encompassing discipline that studies this phenomenon (among other things).
Unitarity rather mind-blowingly posits that the original state of a system can always be mathematically reconstructed backward in time, meaning physical data or history is never erased from the universe. BOOM! (that was my 12-year-old head exploding when I discovered this).
As a layman, you’ve most likely come across this concept during discussions surrounding Stephen Hawiking’s theories about black holes, specifically the idea that information loss may be entirely possible after all. This doesn’t bother me in the least. It’s a heady concept steeped in the conjectural depths of astrophysics and quantum theory. I expect this dialogue to unlock further oceans of insight about the true foundations of the universe.

### 2. The musical argot

We return to the productive musical world of the 1970’s planetarium show, filled with complex modular patching, space echo and bubbling arpeggiators. In this case the modular remains a multitracked Ensoniq SQ-80 and the sequencers are mostly hand-composed and performed. 
Aficionados may detect DNA inherited from Tomita and pre-“Chariots” Vangelis. 

This was supposed to be a small quiet piece, but the b-theme suggested it might build up like a John Williams concert arrangement, so I let the musical muse guide me, and “Lethe” is the result.

### 3. The recording and performance
As with “Chromium Pilgrimage”, no saturation or distortion simulation is being used. The quite significant 70s style audio fuzz is the result of harmonic saturation inherent in the orchestration.  
If I were to arrange this for live performance, I might try reducing it to about 8 tracks to see if a manageable 4-person arrangement could create the larger-than-life sound I’m looking for.

As it stands, this cue uses significant multitracking.  Even small lead lines are doubled and tripled with variations to create some analog drift. For the first time, I have incorporated specific back channel matrixing not for the surround listener, but for the effect it has when merged and played back by stereo equipment.

During the pulsating *forte* sections, you will notice polyrhythms of  3 against 2. The “strings” are playing an ostinato based on threes while the Moog bass taps out a rhythm based on twos. A pair of pre-programed “arpeggiators” pursues a dotted rhythm that (hopefully) glues the two approaches together.
